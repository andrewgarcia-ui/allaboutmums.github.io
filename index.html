Welcome to the World of Mums

Chrysanthemums, affectionately called "mums," are among the most beloved flowering plants in the world. With their ruffled petals and rich, warm colors, they light up gardens just as summer fades into autumn. Whether grown in neat garden beds or overflowing pots on a porch, mums bring a burst of life to the cooler months.

This little site is a small tribute to that beauty — a place to learn what makes chrysanthemums so special, how they got their name, and how you can grow your own.

About Mums

What They Are

Chrysanthemums are flowering plants in the daisy family, Asteraceae. What looks like a single flower is actually made up of many tiny petal-like florets clustered together, which is why mums can look so full and layered.

Where They Originated

Mums were first cultivated in China more than 2,500 years ago, where they were prized in art, medicine, and even tea. They later spread to Japan, where they became a symbol of the royal family, and eventually made their way to Europe and the United States.

Why Autumn?

Chrysanthemums are "short-day" plants, meaning they naturally bloom as days grow shorter and nights grow longer. This makes them one of the few flowers that reach their peak just as summer blooms are fading, earning them the nickname "Queen of the Autumn Garden."

So Many Varieties

There are thousands of chrysanthemum cultivars, grouped into about 13 official bloom classes, and they come in nearly every color except true blue — including white, yellow, gold, orange, red, pink, and purple.

Growing Mums

☀️

Sunlight

Mums love light. Give them at least 5–6 hours of direct sun a day for the fullest, healthiest blooms.

💧

Water

Keep soil evenly moist but never soggy. Water deeply when the top inch of soil feels dry.

🌱

Soil

Well-draining, slightly acidic soil rich in organic matter helps roots grow strong and prevents rot.

🌡️

Temperature

Mums prefer cool, crisp weather between 60–70°F (15–21°C) and are quite tolerant of light frost.

✂️

Maintenance

Pinch back new growth in early summer to encourage bushier plants, and deadhead spent blooms often.

Types of Mums

Chrysanthemums are grouped into "bloom classes" based on the shape of their petals. Here are a few of the most popular types.

Pompon

Small, round, and perfectly symmetrical, pompon mums look almost too neat to be real. They're a classic choice for bouquets.

Single / Daisy

With one simple ring of flat petals around a central disc, these mums keep the classic daisy look that started it all.

Spider

Long, thin, tubular petals curl and drape outward, giving spider mums a wild, fireworks-like burst of texture.

Anemone

A flat ring of petals surrounds a raised, cushion-like center, giving anemone mums a soft, layered look.

Fun Facts

In Japan, there is an entire holiday, the Festival of Happiness, dedicated to celebrating chrysanthemums.

The chrysanthemum is the official birth flower for the month of November.

Chrysanthemum petals are edible and are used in teas, salads, and traditional medicine across Asia.

The name "chrysanthemum" comes from the Greek words for "gold" (chrysos) and "flower" (anthemon).

In the United States, mums are a symbol of joy and optimism — the complete opposite of their meaning in parts of Europe, where they're linked to remembrance.
